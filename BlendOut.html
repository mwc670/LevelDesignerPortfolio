A Stealth Level

Blend Out is the result of refining one of my first ever projects made in Unreal Engine from several years back. My initial inspiration for this project was a 3D level focused on the player trying to exit a house party without drawing attention. In building of this level design, I knew I wanted to incorporate a silly version of a stealth mechanic that would require some lighter visual elements along with some fun capture scenes. One of the highlights of my improvement to this project is taking the idea from a roughly mapped out space to a much more recognizable house structure. After combining some of my Blender work with assets from Synty Studios and ithappy, I was able to make a two-story layout that the player could navigate through utilizing a color matching technique. Add in some of my voiceovers, and the level finally started to come into the vision I had originally planned.

✦ Design Highlights ✦
  • NPC Upgrades: Crafted an NPC awareness system with sentinel features to flag the player within a specific line of sight and to move in a more natural manner.
  • Scene Flow: Added camera recognition for key events such as spotting the player and updated the assets for a better resemblance to a home layout.
  • Character Animations: Added NPC and Player response animations to help build the player's awareness of NPC sightlines and triggering forces.

This artifact shows my early growth and understanding of NPC awareness systems, object placement, and how NPC engagement can be utilized towards the crafting of a playful tone. The original video in the side-by-side shows the cruder figures and blocking that was overly scaled; the updated video shows how I was able to provide better player cues and a more recognizable set of scenery to progress through. One of the main challenges to this design was determining the size of the home that makes up most of this level. I wanted there to be enough rooms that had distinct usage so that more interactive pieces could be added down the line, but I also needed to measure the scale so that it still felt like a normal home layout and NPC and player movement would not feel strained. Another challenge that I overcame was the rigid movement of several NPCs that were following a strict spline pathway. My solution eventually came when I decided on an AI Movement system that focused on connections to other nearby NPCs rather than a predefined area. Once I had the movement defined, the next step was to register a better line of site to keep a level of fairness to the player moving through the scene. I had to test several different solutions, trialing various angle measures that would catch a peripheral glance without bending too far behind the NPC, until eventually I got to a system I felt offered enough of a challenge while still feeling fair. All of this was a big jump in providing a more natural experience that a player would expect to get from an observant party guest. Ultimately, this updated level design now better captures that silly escape mission I had always envisioned.
