A 2D Platformer Slice

Drips of Time, which started as a simple tutorial exercise years ago, has been my chance to explore an idea I have always wanted to develop: a 2D character that is forced through a network of portals, with each one leading to a uniquely challenging environment. Working on this project has forced me to think about all the ways a level needs to express its setting through visuals and effects while still remembering that a solid story has consistency in design keeping everything connected in the end. To that end, this iteration of the project leans into a "biome" type of organization, where certain enemies and hazards are built with the intention of highlighting and feeding off the features of their respective settings. Featured in the video below are these new enemy types that now vary in movement, pursuit of the player, and the range of their attacks.

✦ Design Highlights ✦
  • Indirect Storytelling: Used the 2D art direction for each level to inspire and provide clues about NPCs and environmental behavior.
  • Risk/Reward Setup: Designed level pathways that provided the player with opportunites for extra coins at a risk to their life count.
  • Enemy Variability: Created enemies and hazrads that challenge the player in varying ways, such as: spalsh effect, breath attack, and longer range fireballs.

In the original video, it is clear how I started with only one enemy that did not attack or chase the player, and how the same design was used repeatedly throughout each portal jump. Since refinement began, the core challenge was making each area feel unique as well as a natural progression in challenge from the last. The catalyst became changing the environment first to provide the atmosphere an enemy or hazard would then need to blend into. Once I had the idea for an icy blue level, the idea for the salamander to have a frosty breath attack quickly followed. When I wanted to pivot to a glowing lava zone in the next jump, it meant that I needed the genie to be able to launch out some fireballs. In this way, the player could expect an additional challenge with each new load. The key growth for me through this project was taking inspiration and turning it into something that the player would have to engage with. Another area of growth was conquering the initial roadblock of the player respawn feature. I wanted to have the player not go all the way back to the beginning of a level after losing a life, but it took a good number of iterations before finding a way to respawn the player close to the death point without immediately putting them back in harm's way. Eventually I solved this by tracking the player's movement and finding a load spot in that chain of locations that didn't have a problematic start up. While the original project taught me some platforming basics, this development required me to balance several factors, such as enemy movement and attack zones, the player's attack range, and hazardous effects, to produce a much more engaging experience for the player.
