Willville Little View Drips of Time Blend Out

A 3D Cozy Puzzler Level

Little View, a short puzzler started in Unreal Engine as an entry to a 5-day Game Jam about a year ago, was originally a collaborative effort between me and my colleague Robyn. My focus for this artifact was to leverage elements of the game's environment as solutions for some cozy puzzles. Picking this project back up in the past month, I have had to rely on a lot of the notes we pinned for project ideas that had started to formulate but we were never able to bring to fruition. Fast forward to my current work on the project, and now I am able to utilize my better understanding of collision and pick up systems, how materials can be altered for visual effects, and how focusing a little more on the player engagement side makes for a much more interesting project.

✦ Design Highlights ✦
  • Puzzle Construction: Developed 3D environment that required player interaction to find solutions for each puzzle.
  • Visual Mapping: Used directed lighting and natural pathways to guide the player to subsequent areas without a minimap.
  • Cut Scenes: Crafted opening and closing cut scenes to help provide the narrative "box" for the story.

The updated video above shows how I started to advance in key aspects such as scene lighting, sequences, and interactive elements to better engage the player. This is evident in my inclusion of opening and closing scenes, adding dynamic pieces where static ones had been used before (like the addition of a forceful wind element during the player's final approach), as well as the implementation of ambient sounds to promote the cozy backyard vibe. One of the challenging aspects to this refinement was building upon a structure that I had not originally designed, like the player character interaction system. In order to get the player character to respond correctly to some of my new game elements, like the refined physics grab, I had to take some time to look through the existing code and see which parts I could improve upon and which parts I needed to build anew. Some functionalities, like the player item pickup, were easy enough to iterate on, in this case by including some additional collision boxes to coincide with the existing line trace setup, but some systems, like the save function, needed to be overhauled to account for things like the new collection sets and key asset designs. I also spent time during this update generating more subtle visual cues for the player to follow certain pathways, saving direct text prompts for the moments when the player needed specific actions to proceed. Ultimately, between the enhanced visuals and the increase in challenges for the player, I think this project is better equipped to engage the player while still maintaining its cozy vibe.
